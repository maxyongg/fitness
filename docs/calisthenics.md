# Calisthenics — a beginner's route

Max asked what "more calisthenics" should mean, and said he is a beginner and would
rather be guided than choose. This is the answer. It is a proposal, not a decision —
`plan.md` only carries what he has agreed to.

## He is not starting from zero

Worth saying, because "beginner" undersells where he actually is:

| | |
|---|---|
| Pull Up | 68 sessions · best 27 reps in a session, top set 10 |
| Triceps Dip | 53 sessions · 3 × 10 at bodyweight |
| Chin Up | 25 sessions |
| Hanging Leg Raise | 26 sessions |

He has the pulling and pressing base most people spend a year building. What he has
never done is **skill work** — the held positions and balance shapes that make
calisthenics its own discipline rather than bodyweight bodybuilding.

So: beginner at skills, intermediate at strength. The route below reflects that.

## The ordering principle

Two things decide what comes first, and neither is "what looks impressive":

1. **Load on the right lat.** It is under physiotherapy care. Front levers, muscle-ups
   and one-arm work are the most lat-intensive things in calisthenics, so they are last,
   not first. An older version of `plan.md` opened the session with tuck front levers.
   That was invented, and it was also the single worst choice available.
2. **Which gap it fills.** Core and grip are the two qualities missing from his training.
   The early skills are chosen to cover both while asking nothing of the injured side.

**Skill work goes first in the session, while fresh, and never to failure.** Skills are
practice, not conditioning — you stop while the shape is still clean. Ten minutes.

## Phase 1 — now

Three things. All of them load the core and the shoulder, none of them stress the lat.

### Hollow body hold
The foundation position for everything that follows, and the cheapest fix for the core
gap. Lie on your back, lower back pressed flat into the floor, shoulders and legs
lifted. Start tucked, extend as it gets easier.
*Progression:* tuck 20s → legs out, arms by sides → arms overhead. 3 × 20–30s.

### Dead hang
Grip is the thing that limits every calisthenics progression later, and he has never
trained it — 12 sets of farmer walks in February 2025 and nothing since. It also
decompresses the shoulder, which physios generally like.
**Clear this with the physio first**, given the right lat. 3 × 20–30s, building to 60s.

### Chest-to-wall handstand
The vertical-push skill, and the one that pays a debt: his overhead press has regressed
and there is nothing else in the programme loading that pattern hard. Zero lat
involvement.
*Progression:* plank with feet on wall → walk feet up the wall → hold with chest and
hips near the wall. 3 × 20–30s. Nose to the wall, not back to it — it teaches the
straight line.

## Phase 2 — once Phase 1 is comfortable

Not dated. "Comfortable" means the Phase 1 holds are steady and unremarkable.

- **L-sit progression** — tuck L-sit on parallettes or blocks, then one leg out, then
  both. Core and hip flexors; the natural successor to the hollow hold.
- **Ring or bar support hold** — 3 × 20s at the top of a dip, locked out. Shoulder
  stability, and the gateway to ring work. He already dips, so this is cheap.
- **Explosive / high pull-ups** — chest-to-bar, then sternum. Only if the right side is
  quiet.

## Phase 3 — only once the physio clears the right lat

These are the ones people start with and should not. Every one loads the injured tissue
hard, and none of them are worth a setback.

- **Tuck front lever** — the classic lat-dominant hold.
- **Muscle-up** — needs the high pull-up from Phase 2 first.
- **Tuck planche / pseudo planche push-ups** — the pushing counterpart, wrist-intensive.

## How to know a phase is done

Checkpoints, not targets — the distinction matters, because he has no goals of the
"hit this number by this date" kind and nothing here should become one. A checkpoint
just tells you when to stop practising one thing and start practising the next.

| Skill | Move on when |
|---|---|
| Hollow hold | 3 × 30s, arms overhead, lower back never lifting off the floor |
| Dead hang | 60s unbroken, no shoulder complaint next morning |
| Chest-to-wall handstand | 3 × 30s, ribs down, no arch |
| Tuck L-sit | 3 × 20s, knees above hip height |
| Support hold | 3 × 20s, shoulders depressed, arms locked |

## What this is not

- **Not a second calisthenics day.** Week B's Sunday is the slot. Grow what is there
  before asking for more room.
- **Not a replacement for the barbell work.** The presses and rows are progressing and
  the shift towards calisthenics is a direction, not a switch.
- **Not a reason to add volume.** Skill work is ten minutes and low fatigue. If it starts
  feeling like conditioning, it is being done wrong.
